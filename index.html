What I believe

We have spent decades teaching machines to optimize: clicks, purchases, watch time, accuracy, engagement, efficiency. These metrics have made our systems remarkably powerful, but they are proxies for human goals, not human values. If we want AI to reflect humanity, someone has to decide what it's values are, and make sure that answer comes from the people and communities it serves. That's the work I care about.

2024Personalized Diversity Experiment

Resolving the diversity–engagement tradeoff

More diverse recommendations usually mean fewer clicks — a tax that keeps diverse systems from shipping.

  • Ran a 5-week experiment on POPROX: relevance baseline, generalized diversification (MMR), and personalized diversification (MMR-P).
  • Generalized MMR tanked engagement; MMR-P held it steady while hitting the highest measured diversity. Paper under review.

2023 – PresentJournalistic Values in Ranking

Putting editorial judgment into the ranking function

Journalists' values are absent from recommender design — no method existed to encode them as inputs.

  • Interviewed 12 digital journalists; found they rewrite stories to game opaque algorithms, often at the cost of integrity.
  • Identified timeliness, accountability, transparency, and public service as encodable value dimensions — now coding 4 focus groups' worth of editorial judgment into ranking signals.
Qualitative HCIValue Elicitation
2024Provider Fairness · FAccT 2024

Fairness definitions written by the people they affect

Fairness metrics are usually designed by developers, not the providers a platform actually affects.

  • Ran 4 co-design focus groups with 13 creators and dating-app users; found providers experience unfairness as underexposure, overexposure, and lost agency — none captured by standard metrics.
  • Published at FAccT 2024, showing mathematical fairness doesn't guarantee real-world equity without provider input.
